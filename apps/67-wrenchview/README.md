# WrenchView

**Digital vehicle inspections for independent auto shops — the estimate they approve from the waiting room, line by line, with the photos that end the trust problem.**

## The enemy

The clipboard inspection that becomes a phone-tag estimate. The tech
finds four problems, scribbles them on a sheet, the advisor calls the
customer twice, describes a ball joint from memory, the customer
stalls ("let me think about it"), and the car leaves with three of
the four repairs undone. Independent shops lose their highest-margin
work — the found work — to a communication gap wearing a trust
problem. WrenchView kills the clipboard: the tech taps green/yellow/
red with photos, the customer gets a text link, reads plain-language
findings with the evidence, and approves each line from the waiting
room.

## Who pays

- Independent general-repair shops (2–10 bays) — the owner or service
  advisor buys.
- The tech uses a tablet in the bay; the customer needs nothing but
  the text link.

## MVP feature list

1. **Inspection templates** — the shop's point lists (courtesy 25-
   point, brake special, pre-purchase): items grouped by system, each
   with a green/yellow/red verdict and canned finding phrases the
   shop edits.
2. **Tablet inspection flow** — tech taps through items one-handed:
   verdict, photos/video (up to 60s), measurement fields (pad mm,
   tread 32nds, battery CCA), a note; fast enough to beat paper.
3. **Plain-language findings** — every red/yellow line renders a
   customer-readable sentence from the canned phrase + measurements
   ("Front brake pads at 2mm — replacement recommended; new pads are
   10–12mm") — jargon translated once, by the shop, reused forever.
4. **Estimate builder** — advisor prices the recommended lines
   (labor + parts, tax), attaches them to the inspection's findings.
5. **The text link** — customer receives an SMS link: the report with
   photos grouped by urgency, each estimate line with its own
   Approve / Decline, running approved-total, one tap to submit. No
   app, no login.
6. **Per-line approval record** — who approved what when, from what
   device — the authorization trail that settles "I never OK'd that".
7. **Advisor board** — today's vehicles: inspection status, sent
   time, viewed time (read receipts), approved/declined/waiting per
   line, follow-up flags.
8. **Vehicle history** — prior inspections and declined work per
   vehicle; next-visit talking points ("declined rear pads at 4mm,
   8k miles ago").
9. **Declined-work follow-ups** — scheduled reminder emails at 30/90
   days for declined safety lines ("your rear brakes were at 3mm in
   March").
10. **Exports + DVI PDF** — the report as a PDF for the RO jacket;
    CSV exports.

## Competitive landscape

DVI is a proven category: AutoVitals, Tekmetric's built-in DVI,
Shop-Ware, and Autoflow sell it bundled inside full shop-management
suites — powerful, expensive, and painful to adopt without ripping
out the SMS. WrenchView's wedge is DVI-only excellence that sits
beside any management system: no migration, a text link a customer
actually reads, per-line approvals with an authorization trail, and
flat pricing. The landing page says it plainly: keep your SMS; fix
your approvals.
