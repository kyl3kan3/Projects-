# Marketing Playbook — persuasion architecture for every app

`DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md` governs how things look. This playbook governs **why anyone
buys**. It applies to every marketing surface (landing pages, hosted pages, app-store
listings, launch emails) across all 30 apps. Product UIs stay quiet and restrained;
the marketing surface is a salesperson, and every scroll is a sentence in its pitch.

## The seven laws

### 1. Strategy before pixels
Every app's marketing page starts from a written message architecture, in this order:
- **Enemy** — the real adversary is never the competitor; it's the drudgery/fear/waste
  the buyer lives with (ClipForge's enemy: the Sunday lost to repurposing).
- **One sentence** — the claim the whole page exists to prove.
- **Hook → tension → proof → offer** — the scroll follows this arc, no exceptions.
A section that doesn't prove the sentence or remove a doubt gets cut.

### 2. The product demos itself in the first 5 seconds
The hero is the machine visibly running — not copy over a backdrop. Real product
output assembling in front of the visitor before the first scroll. Claims live
below the fold; proof lives above it. If the product can't be shown in 5 seconds,
show its *output* materializing.

### 3. One unforgettable device

used relentlessly across hero, pricing, OG image, and emails. Palettes are
forgettable; devices are not.

### 4. Copy is the design
A senior copywriter outranks the designer on marketing surfaces. Rules:
- Headlines are claims, not categories ("Publish once. Post all week." not
  "AI content repurposing platform").
- Specific beats clever; numbers beat adjectives; verbs in the imperative.
- Name the objection and kill it in its own section (ClipForge: "Every word
  traceable to the tape" — the anti-slop guarantee framed as luxury).
- Price is always anchored against what the buyer would otherwise pay (a VA,
  an agency, an incumbent, their own hourly rate).

### 5. Receipts, not adjectives — and never fabricated
No "trusted by thousands" fluff. Proof = real artifacts: actual outputs with
timestamps, real metrics, named customers **only with their real consent and real
data**. Until real receipts exist, stage honest ones: the product's own dogfood
output, clearly framed ("From our own 62-minute episode"). **Fabricated
testimonials, fake logos, invented usage numbers are forbidden** — they're a
trust-debt the brand never pays off. Getting the first three real case studies IS
the launch campaign.

### 6. The four-beat motion budget
Marketing pages get exactly **four** animated moments, spent on narrative beats
(the demo running · the device landing · the proof revealing · the offer/math
writing itself out). Everything between beats is static, typeset like a magazine.
Product-UI motion rules (DESIGN_LANGUAGE) still apply: mobile-first, 60fps,
reduced-motion collapses everything to fades.

### 7. The page is an instrumented funnel
- One primary CTA phrase, repeated at hero / post-proof / post-pricing / sticky
  mobile bar. Same words every time.
- A low-commitment exit ramp for the not-ready ("watch a 60-second kit get made").
- Scroll-depth + CTA-click analytics from day one; the trial is engineered so the
  first output IS the sales close.
- Mobile is most of the traffic: the sticky CTA, thumb-zone forms, and sub-2s LCP
  are conversion features, not engineering niceties.

## Page skeleton (default, adapt per app)
1. **Hero** — claim + the machine running + primary CTA + de-risk line ("no card").
2. **The device** — the arithmetic/artifact, animated once on entry.
3. **The math** — the buyer's economics, calculated in front of them.
4. **Receipts** — real artifacts per Law 5.
5. **Objection killer** — the named doubt, dismantled.
6. **Pricing** — anchored, with the per-unit math spelled out.
7. **Final CTA** — the claim restated as an imperative.
